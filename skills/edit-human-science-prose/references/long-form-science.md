# Long-form science guardrails

## Keep epistemic categories distinct

Identify consequential material internally before editing:

| Category | Meaning | Editorial treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Established evidence | Supported by the supplied evidence packet or cited source | Preserve the source's strength, scope, population, and limits |
| Mixed or debated evidence | Findings or interpretations remain unsettled | Keep disagreement visible and proportionate |
| Author synthesis | The author's interpretation across evidence | Present confidently as an argument, not as a finding |
| Hypothesis | A possible explanation not yet established | Preserve conditional language and plausible alternatives |
| Metaphor | A teaching or organizing device | Ensure it clarifies without implying false mechanism or equivalence |
| Illustration | A story or example that makes an idea concrete | Never let it function as proof or acquire invented details |

Do not silently promote material from one category to another.

## Preserve evidentiary force

Keep verbs proportional to evidence:

- `causes`, `produces`, and `leads to` require causal support;
- `is associated with`, `tracks with`, and `co-occurs with` preserve association;
- `may`, `can`, and `suggests` must not be removed merely because they feel weak;
- `in one study`, `in this population`, and similar limits often carry essential meaning.

Avoid vague claims such as "science proves" or "studies show." Name the source, evidence class, or scholarly pattern when the draft supplies it. Flag the attribution when it does not.

## Do not counterfeit narrative

Never invent:

- dialogue or internal thoughts;
- sensory or emotional details;
- chronology that has not been established;
- motives or causal explanations for historical behavior;
- a representative anecdote presented as a documented case;
- an expert quotation assembled from paraphrase.

Compressed scenes and composite examples must be labeled according to the author's documented policy.

## Simplify responsibly

To make difficult material accessible:

1. identify the irreducible idea;
2. replace avoidable jargon, not necessary concepts;
3. explain mechanisms in a logical sequence;
4. use a concrete example that matches the mechanism;
5. retain the caveat that changes interpretation;
6. remove caveats that are merely procedural or immaterial to the reader's understanding.

Avoid caveat dumping. Integrate uncertainty where it affects the claim.

## Protect the book's intellectual movement

For chapter-scale work, preserve:

- inheritance from the preceding chapter;
- the question this section changes;
- the revelation it earns;
- the complication that prevents a simplistic takeaway;
- the handoff to what follows.

Do not improve a paragraph locally at the cost of weakening the chapter's delayed reveal, motif, or argument.

## Treat style as service

Use style to:

- make evidence graspable;
- create curiosity without deception;
- help the reader recognize human experience;
- carry a cumulative argument;
- keep difficult material pleasurable to read.

When style and truth conflict, revise the style.
