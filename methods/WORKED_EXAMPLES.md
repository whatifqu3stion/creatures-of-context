# Paragraph comparisons

These pairs show what changed and the reason for the choice; we have not tested whether readers prefer the revisions. The first two come from a recorded October 2 pass, the next four from saved versions immediately before and after the October 18 integration. The seventh compares a passage quoted in the external patch guide with the current manuscript; its different provenance is stated beside it. These records are not a complete history of the book.

## 1. Keep the reader inside a decision

October 2 recorded style pass; the after passage is historical, not a claim about the current wording.

**Before**

> We can now ask what would be different if an explanation were true. If interruption is the problem, a protected interval should help. If the task is unclear, removing distractions may simply provide a quieter place to remain confused. If another person's answer is needed, further introspection may have reached its limit.

**After**

> The visit has done something that another evening of weighing feelings could not: it has supplied information about the job. The applicant can now ask about the evening duties instead of wondering whether they are simply afraid of change.

The earlier paragraph leaves the applicant to list three unrelated situations. The revision stays with the visit and the question it makes possible. It gives up a reusable checklist here; the conclusion is a better place for that checklist.

## 2. Give recognition an event

October 2 recorded style pass; the after passage is historical, not a claim about the current wording.

**Before**

> In an imagined neighborhood reading group, a newcomer initially sits at the edge and listens. Over time, someone remembers what they said. Another person asks whether they will come next week. They begin choosing a passage, setting out chairs, or disagreeing about a book with people who know that disagreement will not end the acquaintance.
> 
> Nothing spectacular has happened. The relationship has acquired continuity and a little mutual expectation. Participation no longer begins entirely from zero.

**After**

> At the reading group, our newcomer initially sits at the edge and listens. On a later visit, someone remembers the book they mentioned and asks whether they finished it. The answer starts a conversation they could not have had on the first evening. They no longer have to introduce their whole life before saying something about it.
> 
> In time they choose a passage for the group, help set out chairs, and disagree about a book. Someone asks whether they will be there next week. The question is ordinary. Being expected is new.

Remembering a particular book makes the change between meetings visible. The revision is longer, but supplies something the reader can picture. Its closing contrast is also a reminder that an effective sentence can become a habit if we use its form everywhere.

## 3. Keep a contrast that earns its place

October 18 integration pass, compared with its immediately preceding saved manuscript.

**Before**

> Imagine waiting for a medical result. Being told the exact hour of the phone call can end the uncertainty about when you will hear. Feedback on an overloaded worker’s report can clarify the task while leaving the shortage of hours untouched. A friend saying “You’ve got this” may be kind. A friend picking up the children may change what the evening allows.

**After**

> Imagine waiting for a medical result. Being told the exact hour of the phone call can end the uncertainty about when you will hear. Imagine receiving excellent feedback on work that still cannot fit into the available hours. The work has become clearer, not more possible. A friend saying “You’ve got this” may be kind. A friend picking up the children may change what the evening allows.

The earlier version is shorter and says something accurate. The later version makes the distinction easier to remember: clearer instructions cannot create hours. We kept this contrast while removing others. A ban on the form would have made that judgment for us.

## 4. Make a limitation part of the explanation

October 18 integration pass, compared with its immediately preceding saved manuscript.

**Before**

> These were observational records: illness could reduce movement, and other differences between more and less active people could contribute to the association. Bringing movement into an ordinary day raises a further question: where does it fit?

**After**

> They also captured lives already in progress. Illness could reduce movement, and other differences between more and less active people could contribute to the association. Bringing movement into an ordinary day raises a further question: where does it fit?

The revision adds an image of the lives behind the records, then keeps the concrete reasons an association can mislead. It is slightly longer. The aim was to make the limitation intelligible, not merely reduce the number of cautious words.

## 5. Recover the point that a shorter version lost

October 18 integration pass, compared with its immediately preceding saved manuscript.

**Before**

> A person can honor someone who died through an ongoing act of care, while continuing to wish the loss had never happened. Whether to describe that life as meaningful belongs first to the person living it.

**After**

> A person can honor someone who died through an ongoing act of care, while continuing to wish the loss had never happened. Finding value afterward gives an observer no right to declare the suffering meaningful. That interpretation belongs first to the person who endured it.

The earlier ending changes the subject from suffering to whether a life is meaningful. The revision restores the boundary at issue: another person cannot use a later good to justify the loss. This is a meaning correction as well as a change in rhythm.

## 6. Notice when a stronger sentence makes a stronger claim

October 18 integration pass, compared with its immediately preceding saved manuscript.

**Before**

> A practice can spread because it is familiar, enforced, or useful to influential people. We also need to ask what its improvements serve. A method can become more effective at surveillance while making life worse for those watched.

**After**

> A practice can spread because it is familiar, enforced, or useful to influential people. We also need to ask what its improvements serve. A method can become more effective at surveillance without improving the lives of those watched.

The earlier version says surveillance makes life worse. The revision makes the narrower point that improved surveillance does not establish a benefit for those watched. The more forceful version is not automatically the more accurate one.

## 7. Reduce qualifications without changing the finding

Quoted original in the user-supplied external patch guide versus the current manuscript. This is a comparison of two available versions, not a claim that one direct edit produced the other.

**Before**

> The result concerned which expressions became recognizable as information emerged. The authors found no corresponding difference in a general tendency to choose the anger label when wrong. It was consistent with experience tuning perception toward consequential signals, although the study could not assign abuse as a cause or follow that learning as it happened. Detecting a posed angry face also says little about how a child feels during an actual confrontation.[^4f]

**After**

> The abused children were recognizing anger earlier, rather than simply choosing the anger label more often when wrong. The authors proposed that children facing physical threat might learn to recognize anger from fewer visual clues.[^4f]

The longer version explains the measurement, rules out a response bias, offers an interpretation, and adds two more limits. The current version keeps the distinction between recognizing anger and guessing it, and attributes the learning account to the authors as a proposal. The study description still identifies the photographs and the comparison groups; Note 4f retains the observational-design and laboratory-task limits. This is less repetitive because the surrounding explanation carries some of the work. Moving a qualification to a note would be wrong if its absence made the main claim misleading.

## Reading the comparisons fairly

Each pair needs its surrounding chapter. A sharper local sentence can repeat an insight elsewhere; a useful qualification can become tiring when every paragraph takes the same turn. We therefore compared passages in chunks and then checked their place in the whole book.

The external rewrites supplied alternatives, including wording we recovered. These examples identify available versions rather than assigning every sentence to a model: the surviving files do not establish that attribution reliably. We have not filled gaps in the revision history from memory.
